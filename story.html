Story · 77 Flinders Lane

A warehouse, rebuilt

Flinders Lane once cut and sewed the clothes of a city. Coraleno keeps the concrete frame, the loading doors and the stairwells, and fills them with beds, bread and a small room downstairs for the people who like a game.

Chronology

Fold the years

Dates are approximate where we are working from memory, a few photos and a handful of old invoices.

  1. 1958

    The warehouse goes up

    A five-storey concrete frame is built for garment cutters and fabric merchants, one of many along Flinders Lane. Goods arrive through a roller door at street level.

  2. 1990s

    The floors go quiet

    Manufacturing moves out of the city. The building sits mostly empty while artists, photographers and a few stubborn tailors rent the upper floors.

  3. 2019

    We buy the lease

    A small team of hoteliers and builders decides to keep the structure and rebuild everything inside it. The roller door stays, the stairs stay, and the concrete is left raw.

  4. 2024

    Coraleno opens

    Forty-one rooms, a kitchen with a hatch to the lane, a long bar, and a basement for the Soak. The gaming room opens a few weeks later, deliberately small and deliberately separate.

  5. Now

    Still a laneway hotel

    We are a short, tall building with a loud door and a quiet interior. That is the whole idea, and we are trying to keep it that way.

Principles

How we decided what to build

We wrote a short list of rules in 2019 and taped it above the site office. It now hangs in the front office.

  • Keep what is already there. The frame, the doors and the wear marks on the stairs.
  • One door, one address. Everything happens on site, in person.
  • If a room has risks, put it downstairs, check ID, and keep the hotel on a different floor.
  • Staff should be able to say no, and be backed when they do.

Come and see the building

The easiest way to understand Coraleno is to walk in off the lane. If you cannot visit, write to us and ask anything you like.
